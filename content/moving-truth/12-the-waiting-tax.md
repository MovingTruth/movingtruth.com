series: "Moving Truth"

---

“Your call is important to us.”

The recording says it again.

I have already explained the problem once. The first person could not correct it. The second department needed information the first department had already collected. Now I am waiting for someone else.

The charge is still on the bill.

The mistake is still mine to solve.

There is somewhere I need to be. Something I was supposed to finish. Someone who has already waited long enough for my attention.

I stay on the line.

For now.

---

We think of waiting as empty time.

Nothing is happening.

But the rest of life does not stop while we wait. The work remains. The child still needs picking up. The appointment still has an end time. The parking meter continues counting.

Waiting does not stop time.

It decides where our time cannot go.

Some waits are unavoidable. A doctor cannot see everyone at once. A careful decision may take time. The person answering the phone may be doing their best inside a system they did not build.

Not every delay is designed.

But delay can become part of the design.

A service begins in seconds.

Leaving it requires a call.

A payment is withdrawn immediately.

Correcting it takes ten business days.

A company misses its delivery window. I receive a much smaller window in which to report it.

An institution already has my information. To challenge its decision, I must find and submit that information again.

One more call. One more form. One more piece of proof.

None of the steps is impossible.

That is the point.

The refund is available. The appeal is permitted. The account can be corrected. The subscription may be cancelled at any time.

The door is open.

But it stands at the end of a hallway long enough that fewer people reach it.

---

Some people keep going.

They save the confirmation number. They write down every name. They call again before the deadline and wait through the same recording one more time.

Others disappear from the process.

Not because the answer did not matter, but because something else mattered at the same time.

They had to choose between finishing the call and arriving somewhere on time. Between attending the appointment and keeping the shift. Between repeating the story to another stranger and having enough strength left for the people at home.

The system does not need to deny them.

It only needs to outlast them.

Then the record becomes simple.

The customer abandoned the call.

The patient did not attend.

The applicant failed to provide the documents.

The person did not complete the appeal.

Each sentence begins after the waiting has been removed from the story.

From the institution’s side, the file can close.

From the person’s side, nothing was resolved.

The charge remains. The pain remains. The decision remains.

Only the person challenging it has gone.

---

This is how a system can quietly shape an outcome without appearing to choose one.

It does not have to refuse a refund if obtaining the refund takes long enough that fewer people pursue it.

It does not have to forbid an appeal if the appeal must be completed while someone is working, sick, grieving or caring for another person.

It does not have to remove a right.

It can leave the right exactly where it is and make the path do the sorting.

The rule may apply equally.

The journey through it does not.

---

Each part of the system may have a reasonable explanation.

The automated menu directs calls. The form collects information. The deadline lets a decision become final. The transfer happens because responsibilities are divided between departments.

Perhaps nobody designed the whole maze.

Perhaps each person designed only one small gate.

But institutions can see how long people wait. They can see calls end before they are answered, forms started but not completed, appointments missed and appeals abandoned.

And waiting can be useful.

Fewer staff are needed. Fewer refunds are issued. Fewer complaints reach someone with the authority to act. Fewer decisions are reversed.

The cost does not disappear.

It is transferred to the public, one hour at a time.

Once an institution can see who the friction removes—and benefits when they go—keeping it is a decision.

---

Someone finally answers.

They ask me to explain the problem again.

Before I finish, the call disconnects.

The call log will show that the call ended.

The account will show that no correction was made.

Somewhere, this will look as though nothing happened.

But something did.

The system is spending my time until its mistake becomes my surrender.

No one refused me. The policy still says the error can be corrected. I am free to call back.

The door is still open.

But if I do not begin again, the record will make that choice look like mine.

If a system knows exhaustion will make its decisions for it, can it still claim it never said no?
