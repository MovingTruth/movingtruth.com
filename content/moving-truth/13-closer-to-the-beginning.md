In 1776, Adam Weishaupt founded the Illuminati in Ingolstadt.

It was real.

It had members. A structure. A programme for spreading radical Enlightenment ideas.

The Bavarian authorities suppressed the order. Weishaupt lost his position. What
remained grew into something larger in the imagination.

For more than two centuries, people have asked whether it survived.

Maybe that was never the right question.

---

There is no documented chain connecting Weishaupt's Illuminati to Bilderberg, the
World Economic Forum, or any modern organization examined here.

A resemblance is not a lineage. A private meeting is not proof of a secret government.
Famous names on two lists do not prove that one organization controls the other.

But the name may be less important than the mechanism people are trying to describe.

What if the thing worth examining was not one order?

What if it was the ability of a small number of carefully connected people to meet
outside public view, shape one another's priorities, and carry those priorities into
institutions with public power?

That question does not require a hidden society.

The modern rooms publish many of their names.

---

In 1954, the first Bilderberg meeting brought political, business, academic, and media
figures from Europe and North America together in the Netherlands.

Bilderberg describes itself as an invitation-only forum for informal discussion. It
says there are no votes, no resolutions, and no official policy statements.

That may all be true.

It does not make the conversations meaningless.

In May 1983, World Bank president A. W. Clausen and Canadian prime minister Pierre
Trudeau were both at the Bilderberg meeting in Montebello, Quebec.

In a [letter preserved by the World Bank](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/202551603268672741-0560011983/original/WorldBankGroupArchivesFolder1670642.pdf),
Clausen thanked Trudeau for agreeing to raise development financing at the coming
Williamsburg economic summit.

Trudeau replied after the summit. He acknowledged that Clausen's account had helped
him prepare. He described the development language he had pressed for and the stronger
commitment that had not moved forward after resistance from the American president.

Pierre Trudeau did not merely appear on a participant list.

He acknowledged the exchange, described what he carried into the summit, and reported
what happened there.

The final declaration did include development-finance language.

The documents do not show that Bilderberg issued an order. The World Bank had already
been advancing the issue through other channels before the meeting.

Montebello was not the source of the campaign.

It was one route through which the campaign travelled.

---

Control means directing the decision.

Coordination means deliberately aligning what people will do.

Influence can be quieter. It means shaping what another person considers, prioritizes,
or carries into the next room.

Each claim requires different evidence.

I had been looking for a hidden hand.

What the documents showed was something less theatrical—and perhaps more durable.

Power did not need to control every decision to matter. It could arrive early enough
to help shape which people, priorities, and proposals entered the process.

Democracy gives us a vote near the end.

These networks can be present much closer to the beginning.

---

Selection is one part of that mechanism.

In 1992, the World Economic Forum created Global Leaders for Tomorrow. Its successor
is the Forum of Young Global Leaders.

WEF records identify future heads of government among the people chosen by the two
programmes, including Angela Merkel, Tony Blair, Nicolas Sarkozy, Emmanuel Macron, and
Jacinda Ardern.

They were already rising. That was why they were noticed.

The programmes connected them across government, business, universities, media, and
civil society before some reached their highest offices. [*Before the Ballot*](/moving-truth/06-before-the-ballot/)
followed that part of the record.

In 2017, Klaus Schwab claimed that half or more of Justin Trudeau's cabinet belonged
to the Young Global Leaders community. The recording proves that Schwab said it. The
public record does not establish his count, and this investigation found no
contemporaneous WEF cohort record identifying Justin Trudeau himself as a Young Global
Leader.

His participation in the Forum is documented without that claim. He addressed WEF in
2016 and again in 2018.

Selection and participation establish access.

They do not establish control.

---

The institutions also overlap.

Chrystia Freeland disclosed her World Economic Forum trustee role to Canada's Ethics
Commissioner in 2019. Official Bilderberg lists place Freeland and Mark Carney at its
2022 and 2023 meetings. As of November 2026, Bilderberg's website listed Freeland on
its Steering Committee.

These facts do not prove a policy agreement.

They show some of the same people moving through more than one influential,
international forum—sometimes as participants, sometimes in leadership.

Then the names stopped being foreign.

It was my prime ministers. My finance minister. My government's money.

The rooms were international.

But the paper trail led home.

---

Canada's government has described the relationship in its own words.

A 2019 Global Affairs briefing said Canada used the World Economic Forum to advance
Canadian priorities, build networks, shape discussions, and help influence the Forum's
forward agenda.

It did not describe Canada as a passive government receiving instructions.

It described Canada trying to influence the Forum too.

The relationship runs in both directions.

Government enters the network to shape it.

The network gains direct access to government.

The public is left to determine where one begins and the other ends.

---

Then there is the money.

Canada's funding relationship with WEF began under Stephen Harper's government and
continued under Justin Trudeau's.

Government records support nearly CAD $34 million paid across six Global Affairs
projects involving development finance, food security, trade facilitation, plastics,
and biodiversity.

This is not a single-party story.

Nor is every programme imaginary or without public benefit.

Canada committed CAD $10 million to the Global Alliance for Trade Facilitation. The
Alliance reports digital systems, border reforms, and substantial savings across its
multi-donor portfolio. An independent evaluation of early German-funded work found
real institutional gains, but also uneven measured impact and weaknesses in outcome
monitoring.

Canada sits on the Alliance Steering Group, which the Alliance says provides strategic
direction and approves projects and budgets.

Yet the Canadian phase-I project had passed its stated end date while GAC's public page
still listed achieved results as `N/A`.

The work was real.

The accountability gap was real too.

---

The gaps appear elsewhere.

In 2022, Finance Canada said one departmental search found no records supporting
Freeland's WEF trustee role. The same response summarized WEF letters connected to
trustee meetings and invitations.

In 2024, Parliament asked whether she remained a trustee and, if not, when and why she
had left.

The response supplied no date, status, or reason. It referred readers back to the
ethics registry.

None of this proves misconduct.

It shows how difficult it can be to reconstruct even a publicly disclosed relationship
after the fact.

The role was public.

The timeline was not.

If relationships formed outside public view can help determine which ideas reach
public power, is voting at the end enough?

---

So does the Illuminati still exist?

If that means one unbroken organization descending from Weishaupt's order in 1776,
this investigation did not find the evidence.

But if the question is whether international networks still identify rising leaders,
connect political and economic power, hold consequential conversations away from
public scrutiny, overlap across institutions, shape agendas, and move between private
access and public authority—then we do not need a legend.

We have membership lists.

We have ethics declarations.

We have government briefings.

We have payment records.

We have letters showing a request made in a private room and carried toward a public
summit.

The network is not proven by a symbol on a wall.

It is visible in the relationships between the documents.

---

Do not take my conclusion as your own.

Open the records.

Compare leadership programmes with participant lists. Read the ethics disclosures.
Search the grants. Check the payments. Follow the letters from the private meeting to
the public decision.

Keep influence separate from coordination.

Keep coordination separate from control.

Record what the evidence supports.

Record what it does not.

Then choose another country.

Follow its leaders, its institutions, its public money, and its rooms.

This was one country—mine, Canada.

What will we find from the others?
